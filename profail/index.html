NOTES FROM THE WORKSHOP

Ideas, lessons & new things

Thoughts on building useful, thoughtful experiences for the web.

DESIGN · 5 MIN READ

Start with the person using your website

A little planning around your visitors can make every page easier to understand and use.

Read article

Before choosing colors or writing code, think about who will visit your website and what they came to do. A clear goal helps you decide what belongs on the page and what can be left out.

Use familiar words for navigation, make important actions easy to find, and check that text is comfortable to read on a phone. Ask someone unfamiliar with the project to try the page; their questions often reveal where the experience can be simpler.

DEVELOPMENT · 6 MIN READ

Small steps to a more responsive layout

Build a flexible foundation with readable type, adaptable grids, and room for content to grow.

Read article

Responsive layouts start with flexible building blocks. Let content determine when columns need to stack, and use grid or flexbox instead of fixed widths that assume every screen is the same.

Test at narrow and wide sizes as you work. Keep images fluid, give buttons enough room to tap, and make sure headings wrap naturally. A few small checks at each step are easier than repairing a layout at the end.

LEARNING · 4 MIN READ

Keep learning by making real projects

Practical projects turn new ideas into skills—and give you something useful to share.

Read article

When learning a new tool, build something small that solves a real problem. A focused project gives you a reason to practice and helps you remember how the pieces fit together.

Start with a simple version, write down what you want to improve, and add one feature at a time. Finishing and sharing the result is part of learning too: it gives you useful feedback and a record of your progress.
